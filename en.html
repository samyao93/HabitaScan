The problem

Three scams show up in almost every case

In a tight market, scammers count on you being in a hurry. Their goal never changes: get your money before you've seen the unit or signed the lease.

The fake listing

Photos copied from a real listing, a rent that's too good to be true, a unit that doesn't exist or isn't for rent.

Clue: the same photos elsewhere on the web.

The fake landlord

Someone poses as the owner, often “abroad”, and can never show you the unit.

Clue: their name isn't the one in the land register.

The illegal deposit

You're asked for a deposit to “hold” the unit, application fees, or a transfer before the lease is signed.

Clue: in Québec, only the first month's rent can be required.

How it works

Three steps, no awkward questions

Whoever asks ten questions loses the unit to someone else. HabitaScan checks without asking the advertiser anything.

  1. Check the listing

    Add a screenshot of the listing. The app reads the rent, city and text, then gives you a verdict: Don't pay, Visit first or No warning signs.

  2. Send the filter message

    A real landlord says yes. A scammer pushes for a deposit or disappears.

  3. Compare when you sign

    Before paying, the app compares the owner's name in the land register with the name written on the lease.

It really happened

Real scams, and what the app would have said

Three cases reported by the media and police in Québec (sources in French).

Québec City · February 2024

A unit that wasn't for rent

A unit in Saint-Sauveur was listed on Logis Québec using photos taken when it was for sale, over a year earlier. A woman sent an Interac transfer worth two months' rent, then showed up to visit. The real owner had never put it up for rent.

Don't pay: money asked before the lease

Source: Radio-Canada, February 20, 2024

Saguenay · February 2025

Paying to visit

A UQAC student lost $300 before even arriving in Canada. In January 2025, a man paid $150 to visit a unit whose photos had been stolen from a landlord, who says misled visitors showed up “at least fifteen” times.

Don't pay: visit fees required

Source: Le Quotidien, February 11, 2025

Montréal · SPVM

The landlord “abroad”

Montréal police describe listings where the “landlord” says they are abroad and demands a deposit by e-transfer before any visit. Variant: they ask for photos of prepaid vouchers, then send a trap link that empties them.

FAQ

Got a question?

The answers to what people ask us most often.

Is it really free?

Yes. The automatic check, the filter message, the owner guide and the budget are free. Only the full verification done by our team is paid.

Will the advertiser know I checked them?

No. Everything is based on the listing, the address and public registers. You don't have to ask them anything.

What happens to my screenshots?

They stay on your phone. If you turn on smart analysis, the image is sent to be read and is not kept by HabitaScan. Details are in the app's privacy policy.

I already paid. What should I do?

Open the app and choose “J'ai été victime d'une arnaque” (I was scammed): it tells you what to do right away (bank, police, Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre, reporting the listing).

Does HabitaScan guarantee a listing is safe?

No. We help you spot the signs of a scam from the information available. The rule stays the same: visit, then sign the lease before paying anything.
