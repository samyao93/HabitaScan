Renting in Québec from abroad, without getting scammed

You're coming from France, North Africa, West Africa or anywhere else, and you need a place before you leave. You can't visit, you don't know the local rules yet, and you're in a hurry: that is exactly the profile scammers look for. Here's how to rent from a distance without losing your money.

Why newcomers are the main target

Rental fraud almost always relies on the same gap: money sent before you've seen the apartment. When you're still abroad, that gap is the norm. Scammers know it and adapt their story: they're “abroad” too, they can't show the place, but they'll mail you the keys as soon as they receive a deposit.

Saguenay · February 2025

Scammed before even arriving in Canada

A student at UQAC lost $300 in 2023 on an apartment booked from abroad, before he even arrived. According to Montréal police (SPVM), the amounts requested usually range from $500 to $1,200, one or two months' rent.

Sources: Le Quotidien, February 11, 2025 (French) · SPVM, rental fraud (French)

Another trap: the rules differ from country to country. In France, for example, a security deposit is standard. In Québec, it is illegal. Scammers count on you not knowing that yet.

Your rights in Québec, in four points

  • No deposit. A landlord can only require the first month's rent in advance (Civil Code of Québec, art. 1904). Security deposits, booking fees, file fees to “hold” the unit or viewing fees are all illegal (Québec.ca, French).
  • A real lease. Leases are signed on the mandatory form of the Tribunal administratif du logement (TAL), Québec's housing tribunal. The landlord's name is written at the top: that's the name you need to check.
  • The owner is public. The owner of any building is listed in the Québec land register (Registre foncier), for $1. You don't have to ask the advertiser anything.
  • A tenant transferring their lease can't charge you anything: no “key money”, no deposit (Éducaloi, French).

Red flags when renting from a distance

  • The “landlord” is also abroad (work assignment, job, sick relative) and can't show the apartment.
  • You're asked for a deposit, an advance or a fee to “reserve” the unit before the lease.
  • Payment must go through Western Union, MoneyGram, cryptocurrency, gift cards, or to an account in someone else's name, often abroad.
  • The keys would be sent by mail or by an “agency” once you've paid.
  • The rent is much lower than other apartments in the area.
  • The advertiser refuses a live video call, or only offers photos and pre-recorded videos.
  • You're pushed off the platform to WhatsApp or another messaging app, and rushed (“three other people are interested”).
  • You're asked for your passport, study or work permit, or a bank statement before the lease is even discussed.

One red flag alone doesn't prove fraud. Several together almost always do. Other scenarios are described in our guide to common scams (French).

How to rent from abroad safely

1. Check the listing before you reply

Paste the text or a screenshot of the listing into HabitaScan. In one minute, the app spots scam signals, compares the price with the neighbourhood and checks whether the photos were taken from elsewhere. It's free.

2. Send the filter message

The app gives you a polite, ready-to-copy message: you ask to visit and offer to pay the first month when the lease is signed. A real landlord says yes. A scammer insists on a deposit, makes up an excuse, or disappears. Paste the reply into the app and it analyzes it for you.

3. Have someone visit for you

Ask someone you trust who's already there (a friend, family, a newcomer support organization, a future coworker) to visit. Otherwise, insist on a live video tour, and ask for things a recorded video can't do: turn on a tap, show the view from the window, film the door number and the street on the way out.

4. Check the real owner

With just the address, find the registered owner in the Québec land register ($1 per document). The app guides you step by step (full guide, French). When you sign, compare that name with the landlord's name on the lease. If it's different, ask for the owner's written mandate (property manager) or written consent (lease transfer).

5. Pay only after signing

The first month, and nothing else, once the lease is signed and the owner checked, with a traceable payment in the registered owner's name. Keep a copy of the lease and the receipt.

6. Still unsure? Book temporary housing

The safest option is to book temporary housing for your first weeks (student residence, short-term rental on a well-known platform, family), then visit in person. You also avoid signing a one-year lease in a neighbourhood you don't know.

Frequently asked questions

The landlord wants a deposit to hold the apartment until I arrive. Is that normal?

No. It's illegal in Québec (art. 1904 C.C.Q.) and it's the most common fraud scenario. A real landlord can have you sign the lease remotely, then ask for the first month. Nothing before that.

Can I sign a lease remotely?

Yes, a lease can be signed remotely. First check the owner in the land register, and make sure the lease is the Tribunal administratif du logement form, with the landlord's name, the exact address and the rent.

Which documents can I send before I arrive?

Nothing sensitive before you've checked the owner and seen the lease. Never send your passport, study or work permit or a bank statement to someone you haven't verified: these documents are also used for identity theft.

I've already paid. What should I do?

Contact your bank or the transfer service right away to try to stop the payment. Report the fraud to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at 1-888-495-8501 (or online), to the police in the city where the apartment is, and to the platform where the listing was posted.
